Density-Based Volume Conversion

Technical White Paper · Version 1.0 · February 2025

The Problem

Volume measurements in recipes are ubiquitous but problematic for CO2 calculations. The fundamental issue: different ingredients have vastly different densities, yet most recipe calculators assume water density (1 ml = 1 g).

This causes significant calculation errors:

Ingredient Volume Water-Based Actual Error
All-purpose flour 1 cup 240g 127g 89%
Rolled oats 1 cup 240g 91g 164%
Honey 1 cup 240g 341g -42%
Cocoa powder 1 cup 240g 84g 186%

Example impact: A recipe with "2 cups flour" would be calculated as 480g (wrong) instead of 254g (correct) - a 90% overestimate of flour's CO2 contribution.

Our Solution

Mealprint uses a keyword-based density lookup system that matches ingredient names to known densities, with explicit exclusions to prevent false matches.

Density Categories

Category Density Keywords Exclusions
Flour 0.53 flour cauliflower, sunflower
Sugar 0.85 sugar sugar snap
Oil 0.92 oil oily, foil
Butter 0.91 butter buttermilk, butternut
Honey 1.42 honey -
Milk 1.03 milk coconut/oat/almond milk
Oats 0.38 oats, oatmeal -
Cocoa 0.35 cocoa powder -
Syrup 1.38 syrup, maple, molasses -
Water 1.00 water, broth, stock -

Formula: grams = volume_ml × density

Example: 2 cups flour = 480ml × 0.53 = 254g

Impact on Calculations

Banana Bread (8 servings)

Ingredient Old New Change
2 cups flour 480g 254g -47%
1 cup sugar 240g 204g -15%
2 tbsp honey 30g 43g +43%
Total CO2 1.24 kg 0.89 kg -28%

Transparency

Every ingredient in Mealprint stores the exact density value used in its calculation. This enables full audit trails and compliance documentation.

For example, when you see "1 cup flour = 127g", we store density_applied: 0.53 alongside the ingredient, so you can verify exactly how the conversion was performed.
