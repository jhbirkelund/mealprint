About This Report

This report documents the complete carbon footprint calculation for {{ recipe.name }}. Every step of the methodology is recorded — from raw ingredient text through unit conversion and database matching to the final CO₂ figure — enabling independent verification and full reproduction of the result.

This report is suitable for use in environmental disclosures, supplier documentation, and compliance with frameworks including the EU Green Claims Directive. All CO₂ figures are expressed in kilograms of CO₂ equivalent (kg CO₂e) and are based on life cycle assessment (LCA) data from peer-reviewed databases.

Calculation Methodology

Each ingredient is processed through a four-step pipeline. The audit table above documents the output of each step for every ingredient in this recipe.

  1. 1

    Parsing

    The raw ingredient text from the recipe source is parsed to extract a numeric amount and a unit of measurement (e.g., "1 cup flour" → amount: 1, unit: cup, ingredient: flour).

  2. 2

    Unit conversion to grams

    The amount is converted to grams. Weight units (g, kg, oz, lb) use standard conversion factors. Volume units (cup, tbsp, ml, tsp) are first converted to millilitres, then multiplied by an ingredient-specific density to produce grams. See the density white paper for the full list of densities used.

  3. 3

    Ingredient matching

    The ingredient name is matched against the climate database. The matching pipeline applies, in order: alias lookup (curated synonyms), token-based matching (word overlap scoring), fuzzy text matching (RapidFuzz), and AI-assisted matching (Mistral) as a final fallback. The method used is recorded per ingredient.

  4. 4

    CO₂ calculation

    The CO₂ contribution for each ingredient is calculated as: CO₂ (kg) = (grams ÷ 1000) × CO₂/kg. Individual ingredient values are summed to produce the total recipe CO₂, which is then divided by the number of servings.

Data Sources

Ingredient CO₂ values are sourced from the following databases, applied in priority order. ClimateDB is used first; Agribalyse is used when no ClimateDB match is available.

Priority 1

ClimateDB

thebigclimatedatabase.com ↗

A Danish database of CO₂ equivalents for food ingredients, derived from life cycle assessment studies. ClimateDB entries include both CO₂ data and nutritional values. Developed by the University of Copenhagen and maintained by Concito, Denmark's leading climate think tank.

Last imported into Mealprint: January 2025

Priority 2

Agribalyse

agribalyse.ademe.fr ↗

A French national LCA database for agricultural and food products, developed and maintained by ADEME (the French Agency for Ecological Transition). Agribalyse covers over 2,500 food items and is widely used in European environmental impact assessment.

Last imported into Mealprint: January 2025
